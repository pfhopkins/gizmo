# polytrope_n1: hydrostatic equilibrium of a self-gravitating gamma = 2 polytrope

An n = 1 polytrope (P = K rho^2, `EOS_GAMMA=(2.0)`) set up in exact hydrostatic equilibrium
with MFM hydro and tree self-gravity, started at rest. The correct answer is that nothing
happens: tree gravity must balance the MFM pressure gradient. Gravity and hydro are each
compared with a closed-form solution, so a failure points at one of them.

## Analytic solution

Code units G = M = R = 1, xi = pi r / R (Lane-Emden n = 1, theta = sin(xi)/xi):

| quantity | formula | value |
|---|---|---|
| density | rho(r) = rho_c sin(xi)/xi | rho_c = pi M/(4 R^3) = pi/4 |
| enclosed mass | M(r) = M [sin(xi) - xi cos(xi)] / pi | M(R) = 1 |
| polytropic constant | K = 2 G R^2 / pi (from R = pi sqrt(K/(2 pi G))) | 2/pi |
| specific internal energy | u = K rho^(gamma-1)/(gamma-1) = K rho | |
| gravity | g(r) = -G M(r)/r^2 | |
| hydro acceleration | -grad(P)/rho = -2 K drho/dr = -g(r) | |
| potential | phi(r) = -G M/R - 2 K rho(r) | |
| potential energy | W = -3/(5-n) G M^2/R | -3/4 |
| thermal energy | U = -W / (3 (gamma-1)) (virial theorem) | 1/4 |
| central sound speed | c_s = sqrt(2 K rho_c) | 1 |

Timescales: dynamical time sqrt(R^3/GM) = 1; centre-to-surface sound crossing
int dr/c_s = 1.88; fundamental radial pulsation period about 3.2 (adiabatic wave equation
with Gamma_1 = 2; the homologous-mode estimate omega^2 = (3 Gamma - 4)|W|/I gives the same to
1%). `TimeMax = 4` covers one pulsation period and two sound crossings; the `entropic_faces` and
`enforce_adiabat` variants run to 10 (see "Why the star boils" below).
`make_polytrope_n1_ics.check_analytic_identities()` verifies M(R), U, W, the virial relation
and the hydrostatic balance by quadrature, and the test calls it before every run.

## Initial conditions

`make_polytrope_n1_ics.py` is generated by `_ensure_ic()` at test time (deterministic, under 0.1 s;
the IC is not committed; `IC_VERSION` in the generator forces regeneration when the construction
changes). N = 2^15 equal-mass cells, m = M/N, on concentric Fibonacci-sphere shells:

1. with the local spacing l(r) = (m/rho(r))^(1/3), shell boundaries are equally spaced in
   s(r) = int_0^r dr'/l (about one spacing per shell; s(R) is finite since rho -> 0 only linearly).
   The first boundary encloses one particle's mass, and that particle sits at the centre. The
   step is rescaled by under 2% so that a whole number of shells (24) reaches the surface;
2. the particle count at each boundary is the analytic M(r)/m rounded to an integer, so every
   shell carries exactly its analytic mass interval (to within one particle) and the shell masses
   follow M(r). A shell's particles sit at the mass-weighted mean radius of that interval, on a
   golden-angle (Fibonacci) spiral, with an independent seeded random rotation per shell. Shell
   thickness over tangential spacing sqrt(4 pi r^2/N_k) is 0.95-0.99;
3. u = K rho_analytic(r), zero velocities, centre of mass at the origin, non-periodic.

Options (all off by default): `q_max < 1` stops the regular shell walk at that enclosed mass
fraction and puts the rest of the mass in one final shell; the total mass and the analytic solution
are unchanged. `q_max = 0.99` is worse (95-100% band 19% kernel-density rms instead of 4%). `jitter`
adds a uniform per-axis displacement in units of l. `relax_iters` moves cells down the gradient of
rho_kernel/rho_analytic; it only removes the -0.3% mean kernel bias, and does so by moving mass
radially away from the analytic M(r) (90% Lagrange radius -0.4%, U +0.15%), so it stays off.

`python make_polytrope_n1_ics.py --out X.hdf5 [--snapshot output/snapshot_000.hdf5]` prints the
diagnostics of `validate_ics()`: per enclosed-mass band, the per-cell rms of rho_kernel/rho - 1,
where rho_kernel is a cubic-spline kernel density with 32 effective neighbours computed as GIZMO's
density loop does, and the radial/tangential spacing ratio from the Voronoi cell's second moments.
On the previous IC's own t=0 GIZMO snapshot (the `baseline` variant), the offline kernel density
matches GIZMO's `Density` to 1e-5 rms inside 80% of the mass, 6e-4 at 80-90%, and 2-6% beyond,
where GIZMO raises the neighbour number of one-sided surface kernels.

The Voronoi ratio of the old lattice is well below its deformation ratio (1.37 at 50%, 2.6 at 90%):
a cubic lattice stretched along a generic direction is still a fairly compact lattice locally. Its
kernel density was nonetheless poor, which is the quantity that matters here.

Properties worth knowing when reading the results:

- The shell kernel density is biased low by 0.3% (0.5% at 80-90%), nearly uniformly; the scatter
  about that is 0.2%. The previous lattice (cubic, 0.02 jitter, radially stretched by rank to the
  mass profile) was biased high by up to +7.6% at 90-95% and noisier everywhere. GIZMO's own
  t=0 force balance on the shell IC is within 0.1% of zero over 5-80% of the mass.

## Why the star boils (measured 2026-11-05)

With the default ideal-gas MFM energy equation the envelope expands from t=0 (r99 +8% at t=1,
+58% at t=4, +163% at t=10) and after t ~ 4.5 the interior disassembles (by t=10: r10 -6%, r50 -7%,
r90 +13%, v_rms 10% of c_s), alike for baseline, ags and tidal_adaptive (crk_faces and
surface_volcorr differ only in degree) and for two different ICs. The cause is spurious heat transport, not the surface force imbalance:

- **Energy moves between layers at rest.** Per-cell entropy A = u/rho (matched by ID): by t=1,
  before any motion (v_rms 1% of c_s, r/r0 = 1.00), the 70-80% mass layer has lost 20% of its
  entropy (median ln A -0.21; 50-70%: -0.10) and the outer 5% has gained a factor 4.6 (+1.52); the outer 5% holds 3.4x
  the thermal energy of the adiabat K rho^(gamma-1) at t=1, 13x at t=4, 35x at t=10, while the
  60-80% layers drop to 0.83x and 0.61x. The 0-50% mass is untouched until convection reaches it.
  Total energy is conserved to 1e-3: this is internal energy pumped outward. The n=1 polytrope is
  isentropic, so the cooled 50-80% layer now has lower entropy than the gas below it, and the
  interior convects (v_rms grows from t ~ 4.5, cells from the inner 10% end up at
  2.4x their initial radius by t=10).
- **Mechanism.** In MFM (hydro/hydro_core_meshless.h) a face with |S_M| below
  epsilon_entropic_eos_big (0.6 with self-gravity, hydro/hydro_evaluate.h) replaces the Riemann
  energy flux by the PdV form, except where 0.01 < |S_M|/c_s and the Riemann flux moves energy from
  the higher-P/rho side to the lower one (or within bounds): then the Riemann flux is kept. At rest,
  S_M comes only from the mismatch of the two reconstructed face pressures, which is large in the
  outer layers (one-sided kernels, steep profile). The switch keeps mismatches of one sign and
  zeroes the other, i.e. it rectifies reconstruction noise into a down-gradient (hot interior to
  cool envelope) heat flux. This is upstream GIZMO logic, not a port defect.
- **Proof.** `FORCE_ENTROPIC_EOS_BELOW=(0.6)` sets epsilon_entropic_eos_small = 0.6 and leaves
  epsilon_entropic_eos_big at 0.5 (the self-gravity default is then skipped), so every face with
  |S_M| < 0.5 min(c_i, c_j) takes the PdV flux. Same IC, flags and params, t=10: r10/r50/r90/r99
  within 1.6% of t=0 at all times (oscillating with the fundamental mode), v_rms 1% of c_s, per-cell
  entropy within 1.3% in every band over 5-90% of the mass (+3% in the outer 5%), outer-5% thermal
  energy within 3% of the adiabat, E conserved to 1e-4. `EOS_ENFORCE_ADIABAT` (u reset to K rho
  each step) gives the same: radii within 1.5%, v_rms 1% of c_s. These are the `entropic_faces` and
  `enforce_adiabat` variants, run to TimeMax 10; the others stop at 4 (one pulsation period), before
  the convection the heat flux triggers reaches the interior.

What the free surface itself does at t=0 (measured with a temporary output of the face-closure residual sum_j A_ij, per IC shell from the surface;
the shells are one spacing apart, H is about two spacings):

| shell r | enclosed mass | (R-r)/H | kernel density bias | \|sum_j A_ij\| / 4 pi H^2 | net radial force / \|g\| |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.709 | 0.73 | 3.1 | -0.4% | 5e-4 | +3.7% |
| 0.758 | 0.81 | 2.4 | -0.4% | 9e-4 | -2.4% |
| 0.811 | 0.89 | 1.7 | -0.5% | 3.4e-3 | -8.8% |
| 0.870 | 0.96 | 1.0 | +0.1% | 1.2e-3 | +13.8% |
| 0.937 | 1.00 | 0.4 | +3.9% | 3.1e-2 | +6.6% |

(interior |sum A|/4 pi H^2 = 3-4e-4.) Faces fail to close only in the outermost shell, and there
the unclosed area at the cell's own pressure, P_i sum_j A_ij / m_i, is +55% of |g| against a force
excess of +6.6%: the missing face is the vacuum boundary and is mostly physical. The +-9-14% errors
one to two shells further in, where the faces close to 1e-3, are one-sided-kernel gradient errors.
The kernel density bias (+4% in the outer shell) is a 4% pressure error in the outer 4% of the mass.
DesNumNgb 48/64 (cubic spline allows 30-64): outermost-shell closure 0.022/0.017, density bias
+4.6%/+5.5%, net force in the 90-95% band +19%/+22%, r99 at t=1 +10%/+11% (32: +8%), entropy
transfer unchanged; not a lever. With the heat flux off, these force errors only drive the
fundamental-mode oscillation above.

A truncated star (q_cut 0.95, r_cut 0.895) in an isothermal hydrostatic ambient (f = 0.1 density
contrast, m_amb = 0.1 m, u_amb = 0.575, periodic 5^3 box with `GRAVITY_NOT_PERIODIC`, 116k ambient
cells holding 0.354 M; `make_polytrope_n1_ics.py --ambient-q-cut 0.95`) removes the surface force
error (t=0 net force +2.4% at 90-100% of the star) but not the heat flux: the 50-80% layers still
lose 20% of their entropy by t=2 and the star's outer 10% gains ln A +0.4-0.7 (+1.0 by t=10), the interior convects
from t ~ 5, and at t=10 r50 -2.7%, r90 +3.1%, v_rms 9.5% of c_s, for 4.5x the cells and 2.5x the
cost (986 s vs 402 s at 4x2). Not used.

The two options that exist for free surfaces were tried, on both ICs, run to t=10:

| variant | t=0 kernel density bias (60-80 / 80-90 / 90-95 / 95-100% mass) | t=0 net force, outer 2% | r50, r90 at t=10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | -0.4% / -0.4% / +0.1% / +3.9% | +6.6% | -6.9%, +12.6% |
| `HYDRO_KERNEL_SURFACE_VOLCORR` | **+4.3% / +10.7% / +20% / +75%** | +0.3% | -5.2%, +7.2% |
| `KERNEL_CRK_FACES` | same as baseline | **+33%** | -6.7%, +8.7% |

`HYDRO_KERNEL_SURFACE_VOLCORR` (Reinhardt & Stadel 2017) keys on the asymmetry of the neighbour
distribution inside the kernel. A density gradient produces that asymmetry just as a free surface
does, and the fit was calibrated on constant-density bodies, so on a stratified star it inflates
the density (and hence the pressure) of the outer 40% of the mass by far more than the real
surface bias. It flattens the outermost force imbalance only as a side effect. The variant is
`xfail(strict)` on the new kernel-density check so that a fix is noticed. `KERNEL_CRK_FACES`
halves the t=0 pressure-gradient noise in the interior (binned hydro L1 1.1% vs 1.5%, per-cell
net 8% vs 17% of the peak gravity) but makes the surface imbalance worse.
- The IC is layered: a raw radial histogram jumps by a whole shell (2-3% of the mass) at each
  shell radius. The test's shell densities and Lagrange radii therefore come from
  `smoothed_enclosed_mass()`, which spreads each cell's mass over one local spacing in radius;
  on the shell IC this recovers the analytic Lagrange radii to 0.1%, and on an unlayered
  distribution it agrees with the raw values (0.05% on the previous run's final snapshot).

## Parameters

`SofteningGas = 0.021` (Plummer-equivalent; kernel support 2.8x = 0.06) is about half the mean
interparticle spacing at the half-mass radius, (m/rho(r_h))^(1/3) = 0.043, and comparable to the
hydro kernel radius (0.07 at the centre, 0.08 at r_h). `MinGasKernelRadiusFractional 0` stops
the `ags` variant from flooring the kernel at the softening support. `ErrTolTheta 0.5`
(Barnes-Hut opening, which is what the t=0 force uses) and `ErrTolForceAcc 0.0025` (relative
criterion afterwards). `CourantFac 0.2`. Snapshots every 0.5.

## Checks

All of them are computed before any assertion fails, so a single run prints every metric.
**Every tolerance is provisional: none has been tuned against a run.** Radial bins are 12
analytic shells between 5% and 90% enclosed mass. The inner 5% has too few cells per shell,
and the outer 10% is the surface. Radii are measured from the centre of mass of each snapshot.

| check | sensitive to |
|---|---|
| (a) t=0 `Acceleration` (gravity only, since `OUTPUT_HYDROACCELERATION` is also set; see `file_io/io.cc` IO_ACCEL) vs g(r), binned relative L1 of the radial component; tangential/radial rms printed | tree-force accuracy, softening (fixed vs AGS kernel) |
| (a) t=0 `HydroAcceleration` vs -2 K grad(rho), binned relative L1 | MFM pressure-gradient accuracy on the shell arrangement |
| (a) t=0 net radial acceleration (sum of the two) / \|g\|, binned L1; per-cell rms of \|a_net\| over the peak GM(r)/r^2 = 1.37 printed | gravity-hydro balance, including AGS softening consistency with the hydro kernel |
| (a) t=0 shell density (smoothed mass profile, see above) vs analytic shell mean (log L1) | IC read-in |
| (b) 10/50/90% Lagrange radii (smoothed mass profile) at TimeMax vs t=0 | secular expansion or contraction, e.g. under skipped tree walks (`tidal_adaptive`) |
| (b) interior (inside 90% of the mass) mass-weighted v_rms at TimeMax / c_s,central | spurious velocity noise, lattice relaxation that never settles |
| (b) final shell density vs analytic (log L1) | profile drift |
| (b) per-cell entropy drift: max over t=0 mass bands (5-90%) of \|median ln((u/rho)_f/(u/rho)_0)\|, matched by ID; asserted (< 0.05) only for `entropic_faces` and `sph` (measured 0.009 at t=4, 0.013 at t=10; sph 0.032), printed for the rest (0.54 for baseline at t=4) | spurious heat exchange between layers |
| (c) total energy (thermal + kinetic + 0.5 sum m phi) conserved, via `assert_energy_conserved(include_potential=True)` | spurious heating, gravity-hydro energy coupling |
| (c) U(0) = 1/4 and W(0) = 0.5 sum m phi = -3/4 | IC thermal energy, `OUTPUT_POTENTIAL` conventions |
| (d) centre-of-mass drift (`momentum_drift_common`), sanity ceiling only | correlated tree-force errors |

The snapshot at t=0 is written after the startup force computation in `run()`, so its
accelerations are those the code used for the first step.
